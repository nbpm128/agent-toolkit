# Strategy: discussion

> Elicit a design intent through conversation when the user has no existing page or screenshot to
> start from. Ends with one named, specific reference — never a mood board of adjectives.

## Warm-up

Open with a framing question shaped like this, adapted from Jobs-to-be-done interviewing:

> "When [a specific situation — e.g. 'someone lands on this page after clicking an email link'],
> what are you trying to accomplish, and how should it feel when it works?"

Fill in the bracket with the user's own answer before moving on. A vague reply ("make it nice",
"just something modern") is not an answer to build from — ask once more, narrower: "nice compared
to what you've seen recently — can you point at one thing?" If the second answer is still vague,
stop narrowing here and move to Core exploration below — its questions are built to force a
specific answer where the warm-up's own follow-up didn't land one.

## Core exploration

Ask 2-3 questions aimed at surfacing one concrete reference point, never an adjective:

- "Name one object, place, publication, or era this should feel like — not a mood, a specific
  thing I could look up a picture of."
- "What should someone notice first when this loads?" (not "should the hero section be bold?" —
  a question that already implies its own answer teaches nothing).
- "Is there an existing product, app, or brand whose overall feel you'd want to match, even if
  nothing else about it applies here? What specifically about it?"

Keep every question open enough that it doesn't presuppose the answer — a question a user can only
agree or disagree with (a yes/no framed around your own guess) is a leading question and defeats
the point of asking at all.

## Confirm the constraints

Once a reference is named, ask directly what it rules out: "given [the named reference], what
would break it if we did it anyway?" The answer seeds the eventual `## Do's and Don'ts` section
directly — a reference is a point, not a region (`references/philosophy-and-uniqueness.md`), and
naming what it excludes is how that idea becomes concrete rather than aspirational.

## Wrap-up

Restate the chosen reference, the situation/goal from the warm-up, and the constraint(s) from the
previous step back to the user in one or two sentences, and get explicit confirmation before
drafting anything. A reference misheard here propagates through every token and every section —
catching it now is far cheaper than revising after drafting.

## Handoff to drafting

Before moving to drafting `DESIGN.md`, this strategy must have produced:

1. One named, specific reference (not an adjective).
2. The situation/goal framing from the warm-up.
3. At least one explicit constraint derived from the reference (a concrete "don't").
